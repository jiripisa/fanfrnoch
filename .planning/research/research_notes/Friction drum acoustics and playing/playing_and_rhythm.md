# Friction drums: playing techniques, strokes and rhythms (for a rhythm-first synthesized friction drum)

Evidence labels used on every bullet: **[measured]** = numbers taken from annotated data; **[observed]** = field or ethnographic description; **[described]** = player or teacher describing their own practice; **[claimed]** = encyclopedia/blog/manufacturer statement without stated basis; **[derived]** = arithmetic done by this researcher on cited numbers. Read status: **(full)** = page or relevant section read in full; **(snippet)** = only a search-result snippet seen, not verified on the page.

Overall finding on sources: the web has very little written-out rhythm for any friction drum. Most sources describe the mechanism and the custom, not the stroke vocabulary. The only hard numbers found are tempi (BRID dataset annotations, downloaded and read here). Transcriptions of cuíca parts exist in Brazilian theses but are printed as images; the text layer only shows their legend (see section 2).

## 1. Which distinct strokes exist and how they sound

### Takeaway
Across all traditions the basic gesture is the same: a wet hand (or wet cloth) slides along a stick or a horsehair bundle fixed to the membrane. Each slide makes one sound event, and the rhythm comes from the direction changes (push/pull, up/down). On the cuíca a second hand presses the membrane, which gives at least two stroke classes, "open/low" (membrane free) and "pressed/high" (membrane pressed near the stick), with continuous glides between them. Folk pot drums (fanfrnoch, rommelpot, zambomba, buhai, putipù) use no pressing hand; their vocabulary is short versus long rubs, and on horsehair instruments mainly pulls.

### Cited Findings
**Cuíca (Brazil)**
- [described] "The thumb, index, and middle fingers of one hand hold the rod on the cuica's interior with a small piece of damp cloth." "The rhythm is articulated by pushing and pulling along the length of the rod." "The tighter one holds the rod, the higher the pitch." The other hand "can press on the head in different spots"; the instrument can "imitate the voice in the form of grunts, groans, moans, and squeaking sounds" and "can also provide a rhythmic ostinato." (full) — [BYU Percussion: Cuica](https://percussion.byu.edu/cuica)
- [claimed] The stick is "rubbed up and down with a wet cloth held in one hand" while the other hand presses "down on the skin of the drum near the place where the stick is attached"; "The tone it produces has a high-pitched squeaky timbre"; "It has been called a 'laughing gourd'" (cites a BBC h2g2 entry). Several technique sentences carry no citation and the article has reliability tags. (full) — [Wikipedia: Cuíca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cu%C3%ADca)
- [described] Cuíca player Frank Colón (toured with Gato Barbieri): higher notes come from "the correct precise placement of the fingers on the skin and the amount of squeeze pressure on the stick. The closer you place your index and middle fingers to the 'button' in the middle of the outside skin, combined with a tighter squeeze on the stick as you stroke it, the higher the pitch." The drum is held "at a 45 degree angle, the head being in the higher position" so the wetting liquid does not run onto the skin. (full) — [DrumsOnTheWeb, Informal Lessons with Frank Colon part 3](http://drumsontheweb.com/DOTWpages/Drumkeypages/lessonspages/FCberimbau3.html)
- [described/poetic] Brazilian writer's "Decalogue of the cuíca" (translated from Portuguese): "The fingers of one hand slide wetly inside the instrument, gripping the bamboo stick"; between the fingers "goes a piece of wet grosgrain (gorgurão)"; "The other hand stays outside, pressing the hide with firm, quick and gentle touches"; "the outside squeeze with the index finger commands the variations"; "the 'Y' impulse combined with an 'X' pressure creates low and high sounds"; "The hand inside sets the tone and marks the groove (levada)." Onomatopoeia for the strokes: "the so-called *tu-tum-tu tum-tu-tu*" and "the back-and-forth (leva-e-traz), the *fuk-fuk*." The skin is "squeezed and loosened all the time." Playing style: "I play cuíca like someone firing a shot." (full, via fetch summary) — [Blog do Turiba: Decálogo da cuíca (2012)](http://blogdoturiba.blogspot.com/2012/01/decalogo-da-cuica.html)
- [observed] Ethnomusicologist Samuel Araújo's glossary (translated): cuíca is a friction drum whose stick "is rubbed by a piece of damp cloth (water, milk, resin or, as some players prefer, petrol may be used). It allows variation between low, middle and high pitches and between timbres, following the control of harmonic sounds; both effects come from one hand controlling the membrane and produce an alternation between a position free of pressure or under pressure at its most varied points." (full, relevant passage) — [Araújo, *Samba, sambistas e sociedade*, Editora UFRJ 2021](https://pantheon.ufrj.br/bitstream/11422/16566/3/miolo_samba.pdf)
- [observed] Rafael Reif de Paula's transcriptions of the Império Serrano bateria notate the cuíca on three levels labelled "Parciais agudos / Parciais médios / Som fundamental" (high partials / middle partials / fundamental), i.e. three distinguishable stroke heights. The notes themselves are images and could not be extracted. (full text layer; notation not readable) — [de Paula, *A paradinha da bateria de escola de samba*, UNIRIO 2003](https://www.unirio.br/cla/ivl/cursos/copy_of_rafaelpaula.pdf)
- [described] Trovato et al.: sound is produced "by rubbing a wet piece of cloth (often a grosgrain ribbon, as preferred by experienced players) along the internal friction stick"; they name "a classic cuíca technique in which the final note is prolonged" and note that the samba patterns "rely on cyclical gestures"; rhythm can be kept going "through the hand responsible for rubbing the friction stick" even when the pitch hand is restricted. (full, PDF text) — [Trovato et al., Hypercuíca, NIME 2026](https://nime.org/proceedings/2026/nime2026_81.pdf)
- [described] A forum player holds the drum with the head angled towards them and presses the skin beside where the stick is attached; [claimed] a science-museum activity says slower, lighter rubbing gives low croaky tones and faster, harder rubbing gives high squeals. (snippets) — [congaplace forum](https://www.mycongaplace.com/forum/eng/viewtopic.php?f=4&t=1206); [Exploratorium: Coffee-can cuíca](https://www.exploratorium.edu/snacks/coffee-can-cuica)
- [claimed] Pressing the skin gives the high note, releasing gives the low note; the closer the finger to the centre, the higher the sound. Seen only in a search summary over Brazilian blog pages; the specific page was not identified. (snippet) — [Cuiqueiros blog](http://cuiqueiros.blogspot.com/2014/12/percusion-brasilena-fernando-marcon.html), [Som Brasil Pelotas](https://sombrasilpelotas.blogspot.com/2011/10/puita-ou-cuica.html) (attribution unverified)
- [claimed] General MIDI-style kits map the cuíca as two sounds, "mute" and "open" (F♯5 and G5 named in a snippet). These are the GM Mute Cuica / Open Cuica keys. (snippet) — search summary over [percussion.byu.edu](https://percussion.byu.edu/cuica)-related results, primary page not identified

**Fanfrnoch / famfrnoch (Czech)**
- [claimed] Also called famfrnoch, bukač, bukál, brumbál; from the Chod region; a clay or metal pot (often a jug) covered with bladder, with a bundle of horsehair through the middle; "played by pulling the horsehair, and tones may be sounded by rubbing the hair with moistened fingers"; larger pot = deeper sound; used to accompany folk songs, and a popular New Year tune is named after it. (snippet of Wikipedia, page not fetched) — [Wikipedia: Fanfrnoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanfrnoch)

**Rommelpot / foekepot (Netherlands, Flanders)**
- [claimed] Translated from Dutch: a stick is pushed through the middle of the membrane and fixed; "This stick is coated with resin to make it rough"; "The stick is taken in the hand and set vibrating with a rubbing motion. In a sense a rommelpot is also a bowed (string) instrument." Sound: "not very melodious: a kind of 'frroep' sound, which can be described as an asthmatic wheeze"; "foek" in *foekepot* is probably onomatopoeia for it. (full) — [Wikipedia NL: Rommelpot (muziekinstrument)](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rommelpot_(muziekinstrument))
- [observed] Translated: a glass pot with a pig's bladder or plastic foil and a wooden stick in the middle; "when you move your wet hand back and forth over the stick, the characteristic 'rommel' sound is heard." (snippet) — [Omroep Brabant: Lekker rommelen tijdens carnaval](https://www.omroepbrabant.nl/nieuws/2932768/Lekker-rommelen-tijdens-carnaval-trief-troef-tralala)
- [observed, historical] An older Dutch text describes an earthen pot, filled with water and covered with a bladder, with a straw (stroohalm) in the middle that is stroked along wetted fingers and thumb. (snippet) — [DBNL text](https://dbnl.org/tekst/gouw002volk01_01/gouw002volk01_01_0021.php)

**Zambomba (Spain)**
- [claimed] Translated: "Rubbing the rod with both hands, the vibration produced by the rod is transmitted to the skin, producing a deep and peculiar sound"; the rod is sometimes replaced by a rope. Regional names: *pandorga* (Murcia); related *furruco/furro* (Venezuela), *runcho/marrana* (Colombia). (full) — [Wikipedia ES: Zambomba](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambomba)
- [claimed] The player moistens the hand with water or wine, grips the reed (caña) firmly and rubs it up and down, giving "a strong, sonorous and monotonous sound." (snippet) — [EcuRed: Zambomba](https://www.ecured.cu/Zambomba); [ARH Foundation](https://www.arhfoundation.org/spanish-percussion-instruments-winter-holiday-zambomba)

**Buhai (Romania) / buhay (Ukraine)**
- [described] Ukrainer (Ukrainian culture project): "you pull that bunch of hairs with a water-moistened hand ... allowing your fingers to slide on it. You pull up, and then the membrane returns into position." "The pitch of sound changes depending on where your hand stops on the bunch." Used by koliada (Christmas carol) singers; today mostly in Zakarpattia. (full) — [Ukrainer: Buhay](https://ukrainer.net/bugay-en/)
- [claimed] Conical body (sometimes a wooden bucket) with a sheep membrane; horsehair rubbed with moistened fingers; usually two performers, one holding the drum and one pulling the hair (newer versions are held by the feet for one player); a skilled player can get "five or six different sounds"; the name means the bittern and the sound resembles a bull's roar. (snippet of Wikipedia) — [Wikipedia: Buhay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buhay)
- [observed] Romanian field recordings: "Plugusor" with recitation, bell (imitating the ox bell), flute, bass drum and buhai; "Buhai ox sounds from Tartarusi"; buhai "with calls, shouts, and oxen bell and whip sounds from Popesti". The instrument's sound is framed as an ox imitation. (full; no technique text) — [Roots of Rhythm: Buhai](https://rootsofrhythm.net/buhai/)

**Putipù / caccavella (Naples)**
- [claimed] A clay pot or small wooden barrel (or a tin can) covered with skin, with a wooden rod (*canna*) through the centre; the name is onomatopoeic for its sound. No source on the rubbing gesture was found. (snippet) — [Wikipedia: Putipù](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putip%C3%B9); [Visit Naples: ancient instruments](https://www.visitnaples.eu/en/neapolitanity/tales-of-naples/ancient-musical-instruments-of-the-music-of-the-naples-tradition)

**Furro / furruco (Venezuela, "others")**
- [claimed] A friction drum with a stick that "takes the bass position" in gaita zuliana; base rhythm "6 x 8, in the old versions 2 x 4 abounds." (snippet) — [Gaita zuliana (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaita_zuliana); [El Bibliote: variantes de la gaita](https://elbibliote.com/resources/Temas/paises/090_092_zulia_variantes_de_la_gaita.pdf)

### Inferences
- A workable stroke set for the synth, each a separate MIDI-triggerable articulation: (1) **pull** and (2) **push** (the direction change is the rhythmic unit everywhere; on cuíca and rommelpot both directions sound; on buhai/fanfrnoch the pull is the main sound, Ukrainer: "You pull up, and then the membrane returns"); (3) **open/low** and (4) **pressed/high** for cuíca, plus at least (5) a **middle** level (de Paula's three-level notation; Araújo's "low, middle and high"); (6) **glide up** and (7) **glide down** (press or release during the stroke); (8) **long held** final note (Trovato's "final note prolonged"); (9) **continuous rub/roll** for the folk pots ("monotonous" zambomba, "asthmatic wheeze" rommelpot).
- The cuíca's "laugh"/"cry" is not described as a named stroke in any source found; it appears only as the "laughing gourd" nickname and "grunts, groans, moans, squeaking". In the synth it is better modelled as a figure (fast push/pull alternation with rising or falling pitch) than as one stroke.
- Folk pot drums are mostly unpitched rhythm instruments with one register per pot (size sets pitch). For them a pad mode with frozen sounds matches practice better than keyboard pitch.

### Gaps
- No source found that names or describes a "muted"/damped cuíca stroke as a playing technique (only the GM "Mute Cuica" key). Whether players damp the head to shorten notes is unconfirmed.
- No technique description found for the putipù, Andalusian zambomba patterns, the German Rummelpott, or Czech fanfrnoch playing beyond "pull the horsehair with wet fingers". Czech ethnographic texts (e.g. Masaryk University organology course PDFs) returned no readable text.
- The kerosene/petrol wetting is confirmed by Araújo; claims about milk/resin/kerosene effects on sound were not found.

## 2. Stroke durations, tempi, strokes per second, patterns, accents

### Takeaway
Only samba has measured tempi: annotated tempi of BRID ensemble recordings that include a cuíca are about 80–97 bpm (samba), 96–100 bpm (partido alto) and 125–130 bpm (samba-enredo), counting quarter-note beats in 2/4. If the cuíca moves on the sixteenth-note grid, that is one stroke every about 115–190 ms (about 5–9 strokes per second). No written-out cuíca, zambomba or rommelpot pattern was found as text. In the samba school the cuíca has "a model rhythmic phrase" with much freedom to improvise, and is heard mostly in the second part of the samba-enredo.

### Cited Findings
- [measured] BRID (Brazilian Rhythmic Instruments Dataset): 367 tracks (274 solo, 93 mixtures), about 30 s each, 10 instrument classes including cuíca, styles samba, partido alto, samba-enredo, marcha, capoeira; solo recordings made to a metronome. (full: dataset loader docstring; snippet: paper) — [mirdata BRID loader](https://mirdata.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_modules/mirdata/datasets/brid.html); [Maia et al., AES 2018](https://magdalenafuentes.com/publications/2018_AES_brid.pdf); [Zenodo record 14051323](https://zenodo.org/records/14051323)
- [claimed, snippet of paper Table 1] Nominal style tempi: samba 80 bpm, partido alto 100, samba-enredo 130, marcha 120, capoeira 65. — [Maia et al., AES 2018](https://magdalenafuentes.com/publications/2018_AES_brid.pdf)
- [measured, downloaded and read] BRID beat annotations label beats 1–2 per bar (2/4). Annotated tempi of the 11 mixtures that contain a cuíca (file `annotations.zip`, tempo/*.bpm; instrument lists from `BRID - Description.pdf`):

  - Check: the beat file of M4-12-SE gives a mean inter-beat interval of 0.481 s = 124.7 bpm, matching its .bpm file.
  — [BRID annotations on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/14051323)
- [measured] Range over all 93 BRID mixtures: samba 62–113 bpm (median about 90.5), partido alto 87–122 (median about 98.5), samba-enredo 103–140 (median about 129.8), marcha 137–155 bpm. Note: the annotated marcha tempi (137–155) contradict the nominal 120 in the paper's table. - [observed] In the Império Serrano bateria (translated): "The cuícas have a model rhythmic phrase, but there is great individual freedom to improvise"; to move between the 'primeira' and the 'segunda' of the samba all these instruments play a common transition figure. In a Grupo Especial bateria the cuíca section is about 5 % of players (tamborim 20 %, agogô 8 %). In 1933 the "Vê Se Pode" bateria had two cuícas. - [described/poetic] Cuíca phrases in samba "in variations going 2-1, 3-1 and 3-3-6 in the rise of the sambas"; thirty cuícas together "produce a symphonic spacey hum"; onomatopoeia *tu-tum-tu tum-tu-tu*. The numbers are not explained (likely groupings of strokes). (full) — [Blog do Turiba](http://blogdoturiba.blogspot.com/2012/01/decalogo-da-cuica.html)
- [claimed] A manufacturer manual gives a basic samba exercise: alternate high and low by applying and removing head pressure, counted on the eighth-note subdivisions. (snippet) — [Ensoul Dobani cuíca manual](https://manualshive.com/ru/manual/ensoul/dobani-cuic5/2414288?page=2)
- [claimed] In partido alto "the cuica plays [the] Partido Alto pattern" (no notation). (snippet) — search result over [Lumbeat Brazilian rhythms](https://lumbeat.com/brazilian-drum-machine/brazilian-rhythms.html)

- [claimed] Tammurriata is described in 6/8 (frame-drum dance; the putipù's role in it is not documented in what was read). Gaita zuliana (furro): 6/8, older versions 2/4. (snippets) — [Ferraiuolo, Cultural Analysis vol. 14](https://ocf.berkeley.edu/~culturalanalysis/volume14/vol14_Ferraiuolo.html); [El Bibliote](https://elbibliote.com/resources/Temas/paises/090_092_zulia_variantes_de_la_gaita.pdf)
- [observed] Plugușor rhythm in Oltenia is "giusto-syllabic" (a regional folk-music handbook; not buhai-specific). (snippet) — [AMTAP course, Folclor muzical](https://amtap.md/assets/pdf/Szenic%20I.%20Folclor%20muzical.%20Modul%20de%20studiu%20II.pdf)

### Inferences
- [derived] Grid intervals from the BRID cuíca tempi (quarter-note beat, 4 sixteenths per beat): samba 80 bpm → sixteenth 187.5 ms (5.3/s), eighth 375 ms; samba 95 bpm → sixteenth 158 ms (6.3/s); partido alto 96–100 bpm → sixteenth 150–156 ms (6.4–6.7/s); samba-enredo 125–130 bpm → sixteenth 115–120 ms (8.3–8.7/s); top of BRID samba-enredo range 140 bpm → 107 ms (9.3/s); marcha 155 bpm → 97 ms (10.3/s). A synth stroke envelope therefore has to sound complete and allow retrigger within about 100 ms, while a "long/prolonged" stroke can last a beat or more (375–750 ms at 80 bpm).
- [derived] If each push and each pull is one sound (BYU: "articulated by pushing and pulling"), a continuous sixteenth-note line means the hand reverses direction every sixteenth, i.e. a full back-and-forth cycle per eighth note (about 2.7–4.3 cycles/s across the BRID tempi).
- Because the folk customs are processional songs (carolling, door-to-door begging songs), their friction drum most likely marks the song's beat or its subdivision; no source gives a tempo.

### Gaps
- No written-out cuíca pattern (which stroke on which sixteenth, which ones high/low) was found in text. The de Paula 2003 monograph and probably the Bicolor (Paulo de Assis) master's thesis cited by Trovato et al. contain transcriptions; reading them needs the images (de Paula PDF pages around the "CUÍCA / Parciais agudos / Parciais médios / Som fundamental" legend).
- BRID cuíca solo onset annotations do not exist in the published annotations, so measured strokes per second for the cuíca are not available; onset detection on the BRID cuíca audio (audio.zip, 944 MB) would give it.
- No tempo or stroke rate found for zambomba villancicos, rommelpot songs, fanfrnoch carols, buhai plugușor, or putipù. No source on stroke duration (in ms) for any instrument.
- Swing/microtiming of the cuíca specifically was not found (only the general samba fourth-sixteenth anticipation).

## 3. Pitch behaviour within and between strokes; tuning

### Takeaway
Cuíca pitch is set mainly by where and how hard the outer hand presses the head, and by how tightly the inner hand squeezes the stick. Sources give a range of "an octave or more" up to "two octaves", sliding continuously inside a stroke. No source gives semitone values for glides, or says that the cuíca is tuned to the song's key. Folk pot drums have one pitch per pot (larger pot = deeper); the buhay's pitch shifts with where the hand stops on the hair bundle.

### Cited Findings
- [claimed] "The pitch range of the cuica can be as much as two octaves." (full) — [BYU Percussion](https://percussion.byu.edu/cuica)
- [described] "the cuíca can produce distinct melodic lines spanning an octave or more, depending on finger pressure on the membrane, although producing the desired pitch demands a precise and sensitive connection with the instrument." (full) — [Trovato et al., NIME 2026](https://nime.org/proceedings/2026/nime2026_81.pdf)
- [described] Pitch rises with finger position closer to the centre "button" and with tighter squeeze; "once tuned, it's more about the correct precise placement of the fingers" (so there is head tuning, but the playing hand dominates). (full) — [Frank Colón, DrumsOnTheWeb](http://drumsontheweb.com/DOTWpages/Drumkeypages/lessonspages/FCberimbau3.html)
- [claimed] "Changing the pressure on the head of the drum from the outside produces the different pitches and timbres" (New Grove Dictionary of Jazz, as cited). (full) — [Wikipedia: Cuíca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cu%C3%ADca)
- [observed] Pitch variation covers "low, middle and high" pitches and timbres, through alternating between no pressure and pressure "at its most varied points". (full) — [Araújo 2021](https://pantheon.ufrj.br/bitstream/11422/16566/3/miolo_samba.pdf)
- [described] Buhay: "The pitch of sound changes depending on where your hand stops on the bunch." (full) — [Ukrainer](https://ukrainer.net/bugay-en/); [claimed] five or six different sounds from a skilled player (snippet) — [Wikipedia: Buhay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buhay)
- [claimed] Fanfrnoch: larger pitcher = deeper sound. (snippet) — [Wikipedia: Fanfrnoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanfrnoch)
- [claimed] An acoustic study of the cuíca exists (J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 112, 2266, 2002) but its abstract could not be retrieved. — [ADS record](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ASAJ..112.2266W/abstract)

### Inferences
- For the keyboard mode: a stroke-internal glide of up to about 12–24 semitones is within what sources allow (octave to two octaves total range); typical rhythmic playing alternates between two or three pitch levels (high/low, or high/middle/fundamental per de Paula's notation) rather than using arbitrary pitches.
- Since the pitch levels come from hand pressure rather than fixed tuning, the cuíca is probably not tuned to the song's key in samba; this is an inference, not a sourced fact.

### Gaps
- No measured glide size (semitones) or glide time (ms) inside a cuíca stroke; no Hz values for open vs pressed tone. The 2002 JASA acoustic study and the acoustics researcher's notes should be checked for these.
- No source on whether rommelpot, zambomba or fanfrnoch players change pitch during a song.

## 4. How players control loudness and timbre

### Takeaway
Grip pressure on the stick/hair and rubbing speed control loudness and brightness; the wetting agent (water, wine, milk, resin, petrol/kerosene, a wet grosgrain ribbon) controls grip and so the stick-slip character. Sources give only qualitative statements.

### Cited Findings
- [described] Tighter squeeze → higher pitch; too much squeeze can break the stick or pull it out of the skin. (full) — [Frank Colón](http://drumsontheweb.com/DOTWpages/Drumkeypages/lessonspages/FCberimbau3.html); [BYU](https://percussion.byu.edu/cuica)
- [claimed] Slower, lighter rubbing → low croaky tones; faster, harder rubbing → high squeals. (snippet) — [Exploratorium](https://www.exploratorium.edu/snacks/coffee-can-cuica)
- [observed] Wetting agents: water, milk, resin, or petrol (preferred by some players); the hand controlling the membrane changes both pitch and timbre "following the control of harmonic sounds". (full) — [Araújo 2021](https://pantheon.ufrj.br/bitstream/11422/16566/3/miolo_samba.pdf)
- [described] Experienced players prefer a grosgrain ribbon as the cloth. (full) — [Trovato et al.](https://nime.org/proceedings/2026/nime2026_81.pdf); "a piece of wet grosgrain" (full) — [Blog do Turiba](http://blogdoturiba.blogspot.com/2012/01/decalogo-da-cuica.html)
- [claimed] Rommelpot stick is coated with resin "to make it rough"; zambomba hand is wetted with water or wine; buhay hair is pulled with a water-moistened hand. (full / snippet / full) — [Wikipedia NL](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rommelpot_(muziekinstrument)); [EcuRed](https://www.ecured.cu/Zambomba); [Ukrainer](https://ukrainer.net/bugay-en/)
- [described] Hypercuíca authors use gain and saturation "to accentuate the raspy and guttural qualities intrinsic to the cuíca's friction-based sound", and resonators for the "metallic and harmonically rich spectral features" of a steel body. (full) — [Trovato et al.](https://nime.org/proceedings/2026/nime2026_81.pdf)

### Inferences
- Useful synth knobs follow from this: grip/pressure (pitch + brightness + loudness together), rub speed (loudness and roughness), wetness/friction (raspy vs smooth), and body type (clay pot, wooden barrel, steel/aluminium cuíca shell).

### Gaps
- No quantitative relation (dB per pressure, spectral change per speed) found in playing sources.

## 5. Traditions: who plays, when, and the musical role

### Takeaway
Two families: (a) the Brazilian cuíca, a professional ensemble instrument in samba with pitch control and a set pattern plus improvisation; (b) European (and Latin American) folk pot drums played by children and carollers in midwinter and carnival door-to-door customs as simple rhythmic accompaniment to songs.

### Inferences
- For the folk pot drums the musical role is beat-keeping under singing; a pad mode with a few frozen sounds (short pull, short push, long rub) per pot size probably covers them. The cuíca needs the pitch-pressure dimension and fast retriggering.
